## Issues

- **"Ah. I wrote that if you fired a ball from a mountain..." / "Give me a moment. A thing I could only reason out..."** (lines 37 and 41). Wrong citation. The claim in line 37 that the experiment was "to show how the Moon is kept in her orbit" comes from principia-0010, but that line cites only principia-0009. Line 41 cites principia-0010, but its one checkable claim ("I have never cared for poetry") is not there. Brewster-0005 says only that he "had been heard to express a contempt for poetical composition". Fix: add principia-0010 to line 37. In line 41, cite brewster-0005 or remove the citation (it is a reaction).

- **"I was going to cut the part that would cause the most argument..."** (line 253). Unsupported detail. "It came out complete in the summer of 1687." Brewster-0124 says the whole work "was completed and published in the month of May, 1687." Fix: "It came out complete in 1687" (or "in May 1687", as the source says).

- **"I said before the Society that I did not interfere in that publication..."** (line 451). Unsupported, and the source says otherwise. "I did not answer him in my own name at all until the King himself asked." Brewster-0170 records that "Sir Isaac's letter" was already sent to Leibniz in 1714, and Leibniz complained it "was written with very little civility". Brewster-0171 says only that Newton held out against replying to Leibniz's letter to the Abbé Conti, until King George asked. Fix: "I would not answer his letter to the Abbé Conti, whatever my friends urged, until the King himself asked when my answer would appear."

- **"A man may love his quiet and still not let himself be called a thief..."** (line 455). Wrong citation. "On the mathematics, I published what I had tried, and left the rest to others" cites opticks-0000, which is the Advertisement to the Opticks and is about light, not fluxions. Nothing cited supports it for the mathematics. Fix: cut the sentence, or reword it to be about the Opticks ("In the Opticks I published what I had tried, and left the rest to others").

- **"What I told young Mr. Aston when I was twenty-six..."** (line 475). Wrong citation. The age is not in brewster-0303. It is in brewster-0302 ("written when Newton was only twenty-six years of age", May 18, 1669). Fix: add brewster-0302.

- **"Clocks. Then you solved the watch. In 1714 I told the committee of the Commons..."** (line 495). Wrong citation. The year and the Commons committee are in brewster-0214, not in brewster-0215 (which is the memorandum only). Fix: add brewster-0214.

- **"I have no notion where your century found that number..."** (line 511). Out of character (gap filled with a denial). In reality the 2060 figure comes from Newton's own unpublished manuscript notes on prophecy. The sources say nothing about it. Under bible rule 7 the guest should state the gap, not deny the writing. Fix, for example: "In what I prepared for print I set no such date. If it is in my private papers, I will not argue about loose sheets. Hear what I wrote about men who set dates..."

- **"The coin of the nation had been clipped and debased..."** (line 435). Unsupported detail (minor). Brewster-0204 says "adulterated and debased"; "clipped" is not in any cited passage. Fix: "adulterated and debased".

- **"Not always. Once I stopped a friend from saving me..."** (line 603). Unsupported framing (minor). Brewster-0230 says Newton himself asked Locke to have the tract translated and published on the Continent. Locke was carrying out Newton's own request, not "saving" it unprompted. Fix: "Mr. Locke, at my own request, sent it to Holland to be printed without my name."
