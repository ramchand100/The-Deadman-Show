# The last question

Goal: Closing advice, the show tradition, and goodbye.
Target: 3 min (~450 words)

Beats:
- Host reads the previous guest's question (show/next-guest-questions.md) and the guest answers it.
- Host asks for one piece of advice for the audience.
- The guest writes a question for the next dead guest.
- Host thanks the guest, repeats the AI disclosure in one line, and signs off.

## Questions

1. **Adam Smith's question (episode 002):** "Was there something you left unfinished? And if your friends offered to save it from the fire, would you let them?"
   The sources give Newton an unusually rich, double-edged answer:
   - *Unfinished:* the Opticks Book III, "left imperfect" and broken off with Queries for "others" (opticks-0000, opticks-0287); the cause of gravity (opticks-0002, principia-0783); the Chronology, nearly done at his death (brewster-0222); the Lexicon Propheticum, "left incomplete" (brewster-0240); the Method of Fluxions (chittenden-0015).
   - *Real fire:* his little dog Diamond knocked over a candle in the winter of 1691-2 and burned papers of optical experiments; "Oh Diamond, Diamond..." (chittenden-0038, brewster-0177, brewster-0183). He was "not himself for a month after" (de la Pryme's diary). So he knows what fire does to papers.
   - *Friends saving his work:* yes, more than once, and against his own wish. Halley talked him out of suppressing the third book of the Principia (chittenden-0025 + brewster-0124); the Opticks came out only because "the Importunity of Friends prevailed upon me" (opticks-0000); Halley got the Principia public (principia-0002).
   - *But he also suppressed things himself:* in 1692 he begged Locke to stop the printing of his theological tract: "I design to suppress them... My inclinations are to sit still" (brewster-0230, brewster-0231). His prophecy and chronology books were published only after his death (chittenden-0036: Daniel 1733; brewster-0222: Chronology 1728).
   - A natural answer shape: "I have twice been saved from myself by friends, and once I stopped a friend from saving me", then the honest admission that he does not know what became of the rest (he died before; see Gaps).
2. **Advice for the audience.** Best-grounded options:
   - Keep the question before you and wait: he attributed his success "solely to industry and patient thought" and "kept the subject of consideration constantly before him" (reported, chittenden-0041 / brewster-0277). Paraphrase only.
   - Hold your conclusions until the facts correct you: Rule IV (principia-0566) / Query 31 (opticks-0344).
   - Don't pay for a quarrel with your peace: "my quiet to run after a shadow" (brewster-0041).
   - Give while you live: "who gave away nothing till they died, never gave at all" (reported saying, chittenden-0050). Short, wry, and lands well as a Short.
3. **Newton's question for the next dead guest.** He ended the Opticks by handing questions to "others" ("Qu. 1. Do not Bodies act upon Light at a distance...?"), so the tradition suits him exactly (opticks-0287). Grounded ideas (writer to word it; must be answerable by any figure):
   - From the shore saying (chittenden-0057): "What was the smoother pebble you found, and what did you leave lying in the great ocean?"
   - From his quarrels (brewster-0041): "Was any dispute you ever won worth the quiet it cost you?"
   - From Rule IV and prophecy (principia-0566, daniel-0179): "Which of your certainties do you expect time to correct?"
   - From the friends who forced his work into print (opticks-0000, chittenden-0025): "Who saved your work from you, and were you grateful?"
4. **Sign-off.** Host thanks him, repeats the AI disclosure in one line (bible rule 1). Possible host button: the 1731 medal's motto, Felix cognoscere causas (brewster-0273); host may read it, Newton never saw it.

## Passages

Unfinished work
- opticks-0000 (primary, Advertisement I, 1704): Book III "left imperfect"; delayed printing to avoid disputes, published only from "the Importunity of Friends"; papers that "are got out of my Hands they are imperfect".
- opticks-0287 (primary, Book III Part I): "I was then interrupted"; "since I have not finish'd this part of my Design"; queries left for "others".
- opticks-0345 (primary, end of Query 31): "I have only begun the Analysis".
- opticks-0002 (primary, Advertisement II): cause of gravity left as a question "for want of Experiments".
- principia-0784 (primary, General Scholium end): the "Spirit" whose laws he cannot yet demonstrate.
- brewster-0222 (biography, Ch. XV): Chronology "nearly completed at the time of his death", published 1728; sixth chapter "found among his papers".
- brewster-0218 (biography, Ch. XV): chronology "existed merely in separate papers ... in a state of confusion"; handed an abstract to the Princess of Wales on condition of secrecy, and it leaked via the Abbé Conti (Brewster: "rashly put into the hands of a foreigner").
- brewster-0240 (biography, Ch. XVI): Lexicon Propheticum "left incomplete, and has not been published".
- chittenden-0015 (biography): Method of Fluxions: abandoned, late consent to publish, "would probably have been carried into execution had not his death intervened" (Chittenden's "probably"); published 1736.
- brewster-0156 (biography, Ch. XII): none of his mathematical writings published voluntarily; Universal Arithmetic "an unfinished work, never designed for the public", published (it is "said") by Whiston's breach of confidence.

Fire
- chittenden-0038 (biography): winter of 1691-2, returning from chapel, found Diamond had overturned a lighted taper; papers of "certain optical experiments" nearly consumed; his exclamation (quoted below); then de la Pryme's diary (3 Feb 1692): a book "of colours and light, established upon thousands of experiments which he had been twenty years of making", burnt while he was at chapel; "every one thought he would have run mad, he was so troubled thereat that he was not himself for a month after".
- brewster-0177 (biography, Ch. XIII): same story, but Brewster hedges: papers "are said to have contained the labours of many years"; "it has been stated" he exclaimed; and notes "Newton never refers to the experiments which he is said to have lost on this occasion, and his nephew, Mr. Conduit, makes no allusion to the event itself." Important caveat.
- brewster-0183 (biography): full de la Pryme diary text (Brewster's version: "many hundred of pounds").
- brewster-0184 (biography): Brewster dates the fire before 3 Jan/Feb 1692 and argues the "run mad" phrase means only he was much troubled for a month.
- brewster-0178 (biography): Huygens's journal (hearsay from M. Colin, 1694): grief "at having lost, by fire, his chymical laboratory and several manuscripts". Hearsay; disputed by Brewster.

Friends saving (or not saving) his work
- chittenden-0025 (biography): letter to Halley, 20 June [1686]: "The third I now design to suppress"; would retitle the book "De Motu Corporum Libri duo", then keeps the old title: "It will help the sale of the book, which I ought not to diminish now 'tis yours." Newton's words; lovely wry line.
- brewster-0124 (biography, Ch. XI): Halley's reply, 29 June, begging him "not to let your resentments run so high as to deprive us of your third book"; Newton "seems to have readily yielded"; Book III presented 6 April 1687, published May 1687.
- principia-0002 (primary, preface): "it was to his solicitations that its becoming public is owing" (Halley).
- brewster-0230 (biography, Ch. XVI): the "Two notable Corruptions of Scripture" tract, sent via Locke to Le Clerc in Holland for anonymous publication in French; Newton, "Afraid ... of being again led into a controversy", stopped it when Le Clerc proposed Latin (Jan 1692).
- brewster-0231 (biography): Newton's letter to Locke, Cambridge, 16 Feb 1691-2 (date from brewster-0230): "I design to suppress them" etc. Newton's words.
- brewster-0232 (biography): Le Clerc stopped it, deposited Locke's copy in the Remonstrants' library; published only in 1754, imperfectly, as "Two letters from Sir Isaac Newton to M. Le Clerc,—a form which had never been given to it by its author".
- chittenden-0036 (biography): Observations upon the Prophecies first published 1733 (after his death).
- brewster-0237 (biography, Ch. XVI): Newton's own words from the prophecy work, on why he wrote it: "if I have done any thing which may be useful to following writers, I have my design." A good closing line for "would you let them save it?": the work was meant for those who come after.
- brewster-0239 (biography, Ch. XVI): Newton's letter-tract on the corrupted texts: "There cannot be better service done to the truth than to purge it of things spurious". Also could serve as advice.

His papers after death (host may tell him; he cannot know)
- brewster-0290 (biography, Ch. XIX): Cotes correspondence and part of the Principia manuscript at Trinity; ~34 letters to Flamstead at Corpus Christi, Oxford; some at Shirburn Castle (Lord Macclesfield); "the great mass of Newton's papers came into the possession of the Portsmouth family through his niece, Lady Lymington"; Brewster (1831) believes they contain "nothing which could be peculiarly interesting to science" and hopes they will be published. (Nobody burned them.)
- brewster-0309 (biography): Conduit's memorandum itself "was found among the Portsmouth manuscripts".
- brewster-0254 (biography): Portsmouth papers include sheets in his hand of Flammel's "Explication of Hieroglyphic Figures" (alchemical; thin, second-hand).
- brewster-0260 (biography): his reflecting instrument for measuring the moon's distance at sea, sent to Halley in 1700, stayed among papers until published in 1742 (Brewster's sentence is ambiguous about whose death in 1742; do not use the date).

Advice / character
- chittenden-0041 (biography): "solely to industry and patient thought"; "He kept the subject of consideration constantly before him, and waited till the first dawning opened gradually into a full and clear light" (Chittenden's words; paraphrase only). Sat on his bedside for hours half-dressed; forgot meals.
- brewster-0277 (biography): "he himself declared, that whatever service he had done to the public was not owing to any extraordinary sagacity, but solely to industry and patient thought" (reported speech).
- chittenden-0050 (biography): giving while alive (reported saying).
- principia-0566, opticks-0344 (primary): Rule IV / Query 31.
- brewster-0041 (biography): quiet vs. shadow.
- chittenden-0057 (biography): sea-shore saying (also listed in 08; use once).

## Gaps

- **No passage records Newton asking for papers to be burned, or burning them himself,** and nothing about friends offering to save anything from a fire at his death. The only fire is the Diamond accident (and Huygens's hearsay of a lost laboratory). Do not invent a deathbed burning or instructions to destroy manuscripts.
- **Diamond is shaky:** Brewster notes Newton never mentions the lost experiments and Conduit never mentions the fire; the two biographies quote the exclamation differently. Newton can tell it as a memory, but the writer might let him be wry about it rather than build the answer on it.
- **What happened to his papers** after 1727 (Portsmouth family, Brewster's hope of publication) is after his death: host material only. Later history (the 1936 Sotheby's sale, Keynes, the theological and alchemical manuscripts becoming known) is NOT in our sources; the host may mention it only as a general, attributed claim and should flag it.
- **His private theology:** brewster-0237/0239 summarise the "Corruptions of Scripture" tract (1 John v. 7 and 1 Tim. iii. 16 as corrupted texts). Brewster says he "has been regarded as an Antitrinitarian" but that this "is not warranted by any thing which he has published", and records that Newton was "greatly offended" when Whiston called him an Arian and kept Whiston out of the Royal Society. Brewster's defence of his orthodoxy is the biographer's opinion. Do not have Newton declare anti-Trinitarian views; the sources do not give them to him.
- **Alchemy** appears only second-hand (brewster-0254, brewster-0178). Not enough for him to "confess" alchemical papers.
- **"Shoulders of giants"** is not in the indexed texts.
- **No piece of explicit advice to young people** exists in his own writings; the advice must be built from Rule IV / Query 31, the reported "industry and patient thought", and the reported saying on giving.
- **Overlap:** Diamond is in 05-personal and the "litigious lady" letter is in 03/05/06. In 09, refer back to them briefly ("you've heard about my dog") rather than retelling. 06-life (Q8) uses Newton's rules for travelling abroad (brewster-0302 onward) as advice for young people; I did not research that passage. If 06 uses it, 09's advice should come from a different source (Rule IV, patient thought, or giving while alive).
- The question for the next guest is the writer's composition; the ideas above are anchored to passages but no source contains such a question.
