# Hot takes on today

Goal: A faster round on modern topics. The host explains each briefly; the guest reasons from their principles and says plainly what is new to them.
Target: 8 min (~1200 words)

Beats:
- Four to six quick topics, chosen to suit this guest.
- At least one topic where the guest's answer would surprise the audience.

## Questions

1. **Satellites and the Moon landing.** "We now fire machines up so fast they never come down: they circle the Earth for years. We even sent men to the Moon and back." (Newton already imagined it: the leaden ball fired from a mountain that "might go quite round the whole earth", and the "little moon" skimming the mountain tops. principia-0009, principia-0581, principia-0010)
2. **Ships finding their place at sea.** "Every phone now knows its longitude to within a few feet, using clocks in the sky." (His 1714 memorandum to Parliament: a watch good enough at sea "hath not yet been made"; the Moon's theory not exact enough. brewster-0215)
3. **Your laws were later found to be not quite right at the extremes. Does that sting?** (Rule IV: true "till such time as other phænomena occur"; Query 31 on exceptions; preface hoping for "some truer method of philosophy"; he never claimed to know the cause of gravity. principia-0566, opticks-0344, principia-0002, principia-0783, opticks-0002)
4. **Online quarrels / social media pile-ons.** "Today anyone can attack your work in front of millions within an hour." (The "litigious lady" letter; "my quiet to run after a shadow"; delayed the Opticks "To avoid being engaged in Disputes". chittenden-0025, brewster-0041, opticks-0000, chittenden-0044)
5. **The end of the world in 2060.** "The internet says you predicted the world ends in 2060. Did you?" SURPRISE topic: he scorns date-setters ("The folly of Interpreters has been, to foretel times and things"), refuses to interpret what is unfulfilled ("Let time be the Interpreter"). But privately, to Conduit, he did think a comet (1680) might one day fall into the sun and burn the Earth. daniel-0179, daniel-0092, daniel-0078, brewster-0307, brewster-0309, principia-0775
6. **Science versus religion.** "Many people today think science has made God unnecessary." (General Scholium: "This most beautiful system ... could only proceed from the counsel and dominion of an intelligent and powerful Being"; discoursing of God "does certainly belong to Natural Philosophy"; rebuke to Halley. principia-0779, principia-0783, opticks-0315, brewster-0285)
7. **Planets around other stars.** "We have found thousands of planets circling other suns." (General Scholium on fixed stars as centres of "other like systems"; Query 31 on God making "Worlds of several sorts"; to Conduit he took the fixed stars to be suns enlightening other planets. principia-0779, opticks-0344, brewster-0308)
8. **Machines that do mathematics (AI).** Optional, thinly grounded: "the errors are not in the art, but in the artificers" (a perfect mechanic would be the most perfect geometer); he solved Leibniz's 1716 challenge problem in one evening after a day at the Mint. principia-0000, principia-0001, brewster-0156, chittenden-0040. See Gaps.
9. **Money / inflation / cryptocurrency.** Only weakly grounded: he ran the recoinage of a debased currency. chittenden-0040, brewster-0207. See Gaps.

## Passages

Satellites, orbits, the Moon
- principia-0009 (primary, Principia Book I, Definition V): the leaden ball fired from a mountain top with gunpowder; faster and faster until it "might go quite round the whole earth before it falls" or go off "into the celestial spaces". The best orbit/satellite passage we have.
- principia-0010 (primary, Book I, Definition V): the Moon is held in orbit the same way; too small a force and it flies off, too great and it is drawn down; "it belongs to the mathematicians to find the force" for a given orbit and velocity.
- principia-0581 (primary, Book III, Scholium): a "little moon" so low it almost touches the tops of the highest mountains would, if stopped, fall at the same speed as heavy bodies there; so the Moon's force and gravity have one cause (Rules I and II). Works as "low Earth orbit" in his terms.
- brewster-0215 (biography, Ch. XIV): Newton's own memorandum read to the Commons committee on longitude, 1714 (four methods: a sea watch "hath not yet been made"; Jupiter's satellites cannot be observed from a moving ship; the Moon's theory good only "within two or three degrees"; Ditton's scheme). Newton's words, quotable.
- brewster-0214, brewster-0216 (biography, Ch. XIV): context: 1714 petition by ship captains and owners; Newton, Halley, Cotes and Clarke examined; the Act passed in July 1714. Whiston's (hostile) account that Newton kept silent and then repeated Whiston's words; Brewster disputes it.

Being overturned / revised
- principia-0566 (primary, Book III, Rule IV): the full rule (quoted below).
- opticks-0344 (primary, Opticks Book III Query 31): induction is "no Demonstration of general Conclusions; yet it is the best way of arguing"; if exceptions later occur, the conclusion is pronounced "with such Exceptions as occur". Also God may "vary the Laws of Nature, and make Worlds of several sorts".
- principia-0002 (primary, Author's Preface): he suspects all phenomena depend on unknown forces between particles; hopes his principles "will afford some light either to this or some truer method of philosophy"; asks that defects be "kindly supplied".
- principia-0783 (primary, General Scholium): "I frame no hypotheses"; has not discovered the cause of gravity; "to us it is enough that gravity does really exist".
- opticks-0002 (primary, Advertisement II): put the cause of gravity as a Question "because I am not yet satisfied about it for want of Experiments".
- principia-0562 (primary, Rule I): admit no more causes than true and sufficient; "Nature is pleased with simplicity".

Disputes / public quarrels
- chittenden-0025 (biography): Newton's letter to Halley, 20 June [1686]: will suppress the third book; "Philosophy is such an impertinently litigious lady...". Newton's words. (Same letter in brewster-0123, spelled "lawsuits" there; quote from chittenden-0025 as copied below.)
- brewster-0041 (biography, Ch. IV): Newton's letters after the light controversy: "no farther solicitous about matters of philosophy"; "against the grain to put pen to paper"; to Leibnitz, 9 Dec 1675, "my quiet to run after a shadow". Newton's words.
- opticks-0000 (primary, Advertisement I, 1704): delayed printing "To avoid being engaged in Disputes about these Matters"; published only because of "the Importunity of Friends"; does not want it translated without his consent.
- opticks-0001 (primary, Advertisement I): lent out a manuscript, then "met with some Things copied out of it", so made it public. (Copying/plagiarism worry.)
- chittenden-0044 (biography): Chittenden says he never voluntarily published any purely mathematical writing, out of "repugnance to everything like contest or dispute"; Chittenden's own judgement.
- brewster-0156 (biography): Brewster: "If he withheld his labours from the world in order to avoid the disputes ... he adopted the very worst method of securing his tranquillity." Biographer's verdict (not quotable as Newton).
- brewster-0086 (biography, Ch. IX): resolved not to publish the Opticks during Hooke's life; Hooke died 1702, Opticks appeared 1704.

End of the world / prophecy dates
- daniel-0179 (primary, Daniel Part II Ch. I): the key passage: interpreters foolishly "foretel times and things"; prophecy given so that "after they were fulfilled they might be interpreted by the event"; "the time is not yet come for understanding them perfectly"; "Till then we must content ourselves with interpreting what hath been already fulfilled."
- daniel-0092 (primary, Part I Ch. X): Daniel's prophecies "reach to the end of the world"; the seven weeks are "not yet fulfilled, I shall not attempt a particular interpretation"; ends "The manner I know not. Let time be the Interpreter."
- daniel-0078 (primary, Part I Ch. VIII): the little horn (the Pope's temporal dominion, acquired "in the latter half of the eighth century") has times and laws for "a time times and half a time ... that is, for 1260 solar years"; afterwards his dominion is taken away "not at once, but by degrees". Newton gives NO end year here. (See Gaps on 2060.)
- daniel-0202, daniel-0100 (primary): days put for years; time, times and half a time = 42 months = 1260 days.
- brewster-0307, brewster-0308, brewster-0309 (biography, appendix: Conduit's memorandum of a conversation at Kensington, Sunday 7 March 1724-5): Newton's "conjecture (he would affirm nothing)" that the comet of 1680 would, after "perhaps ... five or six revolutions more", drop into the sun and so heat it "that this earth would be burnt, and no animals in it could live"; that the inhabitants of this world are "of a short date" (all arts discovered within memory of history); repeopling "required the power of a Creator"; and his sayings "I do not deal in conjectures", "that concerned us more", and, laughing, "that he had said enough for people to know his meaning". This is Conduit's report, partly in indirect speech.
- principia-0775 (primary, Book III, Prop. XLII): the comet of 1680 came within a sixth of the sun's diameter, was retarded, and "will at last fall down upon the body of the sun"; fixed stars "may be recruited by comets that fall upon them". The published basis of the Conduit conversation.

Religion and natural philosophy
- principia-0779 (primary, General Scholium): "This most beautiful system ..."; fixed stars as centres of "other like systems", placed at "immense distances" so they do not fall on each other.
- principia-0780 (primary, General Scholium): God "governs all things, not as the soul of the world, but as Lord over all".
- principia-0783 (primary, General Scholium): God known "by his most wise and excellent contrivances of things, and final causes"; "a god without dominion, providence, and final causes, is nothing else but Fate and Nature"; discourse of God "does certainly belong to Natural Philosophy".
- opticks-0315 (primary, Query 28): "Was the Eye contrived without Skill in Opticks"; every true step "brings us nearer" to the first cause.
- opticks-0345 (primary, end of Query 31): if natural philosophy is perfected, "the Bounds of Moral Philosophy will be also enlarged".
- brewster-0285 (biography, Ch. XIX): when Halley said "any thing disrespectful to religion", Newton checked him: "I have studied these things,—you have not." Recorded saying. Brewster also says he abhorred persecution and favoured religious toleration (Brewster's description).
- brewster-0241, brewster-0245 (biography): Bentley letters: the Third Book was written with "an eye upon such principles as might work, with considering men, for the belief of a Deity" (Brewster paraphrases in the third person, so not a clean quote); the fourth letter's sentence on innate gravity and "a supernatural power" is in quotation marks and quotable.

Other worlds
- opticks-0344 (primary, Query 31): God able to "make Worlds of several sorts in several Parts of the Universe. At least, I see nothing of Contradiction in all this."
- brewster-0308 (biography, Conduit memorandum): he took the fixed stars "to be suns enlightening other planets as our sun does ours"; doubted whether there were not "intelligent beings superior to us" superintending the heavens; all planets made of the same matter as the earth "but variously concocted".

Machines / mathematics
- principia-0000, principia-0001 (primary, Author's Preface): rational vs practical mechanics; "the errors are not in the art, but in the artificers"; geometry "is founded in mechanical practice". The closest he comes to a view on machines that compute.
- brewster-0156, chittenden-0040 (biography): Leibniz's 1716 problem "for the purpose ... of feeling the pulse of the English analysts", received about five o'clock returning from the Mint, solved before bed; Bernoulli's 1697 problems solved the day after receipt.

Money
- chittenden-0040, brewster-0207 (biography): the coin "had been adulterated and debased"; Montague's recoinage; Newton Warden 1695 (Montague's letter dated 19 March 1695, brewster-0205), Master 1699, office worth "twelve or fifteen hundred pounds per annum"; wrote an official Report on the Coinage and a Table of Assays of Foreign Coins. No detail of what the report said.
- chittenden-0050 (biography): he regarded money only as "one of the means wherewith he had been intrusted to do good" (Chittenden's words); his remark that those "who gave away nothing till they died, never gave at all" (reported saying, indirect speech).

## Gaps

- **2060 is not in the sources.** No passage gives a year for the end of the world or for Christ's return. daniel-0078 says the little horn's dominion lasts 1260 years from its rise "in the latter half of the eighth century", but Newton does not do the sum or name an end date, and says the dominion ends "by degrees". The writer must not let Newton state 2060 or add 800 + 1260 himself. The honest answer is the surprise: he condemns date-setting (daniel-0179), and the only "end of the Earth" he speculated about was physical (the comet of 1680 heating the sun: brewster-0307, a private conjecture he would "affirm nothing" about). The famous 2060 manuscript is a later discovery and is not in our texts.
- **System of the World not indexed.** The popular version of the mountain-cannon thought experiment (with the diagram) is in Newton's separate "System of the World", which is NOT in our index (the principia source ends with the General Scholium). Use the Definition V version (principia-0009), which has the same idea: leaden ball, gunpowder, mountain, round the whole earth.
- **Modern physics.** Relativity, quantum theory, photons, energy, fields: nothing. He can only respond through Rule IV, Query 31's "Exceptions", and his admission that he does not know gravity's cause. He must not adopt these ideas as his own (figure guidance).
- **AI / computing machines.** No passage on calculating machines or thinking machines. The only material is the preface on mechanics and geometry and his speed at problems; anything further must be framed as Newton reasoning, not recalling. Weakest topic; consider dropping.
- **Money / inflation / cryptocurrency.** The sources say only that the coin was "adulterated and debased", that he helped the recoinage with his mathematical and chemical knowledge, and that he wrote a Report on the Coinage and a table of foreign assays. No content of his monetary views, no text on paper money, credit, or the South Sea Bubble (search found nothing). Mint prosecutions of counterfeiters are not in the texts. Keep any money take thin or skip.
- **Social media** has no direct grounding beyond his dislike of disputes; fine to use, but it is Newton reasoning by analogy.
- **Overlap:** 01-welcome already lists the leaden-ball passage (principia-0009) and plans to have the host mention satellites and Moon landings. 04/06 use Rule IV, and 06 uses daniel-0179. In 07, use these as quick callbacks; the fresh material is principia-0581 (the "little moon" at mountain-top height), brewster-0215 (longitude memo), and brewster-0307/0309 plus principia-0775 (the comet of 1680 and the burnt Earth).
- "Industry and patient thought" (brewster-0277, chittenden-0041) is reported speech in the biographer's words, not a verbatim quote.
- The Whiston story of Newton's silence before the Commons committee (brewster-0216) is Whiston's hostile account, disputed by Brewster.
- Brewster and Chittenden defend Newton's orthodoxy; nothing in the texts covers his private anti-Trinitarian views (beyond the "Corruptions of Scripture" tract title in 08/09 notes). Do not invent them.
