# Regrets and legacy

Goal: What the guest got wrong, unfinished work, and how they want to be remembered.
Target: 5 min (~750 words)

Beats:
- Something the guest would change or regrets.
- How the guest feels about their reputation today.

## Questions

1. **What do you regret?** Strongest grounded answers: (a) wishing a friend dead, then begging Locke's pardon in 1693 (brewster-0194); (b) letting disputes rob him of his quiet: "my quiet to run after a shadow" (brewster-0041); (c) holding back his mathematics, which (say the biographers) invited the Leibniz quarrel (chittenden-0044, brewster-0156). Note: the Locke letter shows him at his rawest; check it is not already used in 05-personal.
2. **What did you leave unfinished?** Opticks Book III "left imperfect", the interrupted experiments on the fringes, ending in Queries "in order to a farther search to be made by others" (opticks-0000, opticks-0287, opticks-0345); the cause of gravity (principia-0783, opticks-0002); the "subtle Spirit" he could not demonstrate (principia-0784); the lunar theory "being imperfect" (principia-0002); the Chronology "nearly completed at the time of his death" (brewster-0222); the Lexicon Propheticum "left incomplete" (brewster-0240); the Method of Fluxions not published till 1736 (chittenden-0015). (Overlaps with 09; use the science side here, the papers/fire side in 09.)
3. **Were you ever just wrong?** He is careful to say what is proved and what is not; on the Chronology he admitted "there may be errors of five or ten years, and sometimes twenty" (brewster-0218). Rule IV makes revision part of the method (principia-0566, see 07).
4. **How do you want to be remembered? Does it bother you that people now call you the greatest scientist ever?** The sea-shore saying (chittenden-0057 / brewster-0284); the preface plea to be read "with candour" (principia-0002). Brewster's caveat: his modesty "was not founded on any indifference to the fame" (brewster-0284).
5. **Your last days.** Stone in the bladder from 1722; Kensington; presided at the Royal Society 28 Feb 1727; died 20 March 1727 aged 84 (brewster-0270, brewster-0271, chittenden-0050, chittenden-0058). The guest cannot narrate his own death or funeral, but can speak of the illness up to his last days.
6. **Host tells him: you are buried in Westminster Abbey, with a monument.** He reacts to what Ram tells him: lay in state in the Jerusalem Chamber, pall carried by the Lord Chancellor, two dukes and three earls; monument with youths carrying a prism, a telescope, a furnace, and "money newly coined"; Latin epitaph; and Pope's couplet (brewster-0272, brewster-0273, chittenden-0059, brewster-0289, chittenden-0062). Host-delivered only (rule 5: after his death).

## Passages

Regrets
- brewster-0194 (biography, Ch. XIII): Newton's letter to Locke, signed at the Bull, Shoreditch, 16 Sept 1693 (date per brewster-0195 heading): he had suspected Locke of trying to "embroil me with women", said "'twere better if you were dead", took him for "a Hobbist", suspected a plot to sell him an office; begs pardon. Newton's words.
- brewster-0285 (biography, Ch. XIX): Brewster calls it "the affecting letter in which he acknowledges to Locke that he had thought and spoken of him uncharitably".
- brewster-0041 (biography, Ch. IV): the light controversy letters ("my quiet to run after a shadow"). Newton's words.
- chittenden-0025 (biography): 1686 letter to Halley planning to suppress Book III ("litigious lady"). Newton's words.
- chittenden-0044 (biography): Chittenden: early publication of fluxions "would have anticipated all rivalry, and secured him from the contentious claims of Leibnitz". Biographer's judgement.
- brewster-0156 (biography, Ch. XII): none of his mathematical writings "voluntarily communicated to the world by himself"; Universal Arithmetic published (said to be) by Whiston's breach of confidence, "an unfinished work"; Quadrature of Curves forced out by "plagiarisms from the manuscripts ... lent to his friends". Brewster speculates about motives ("It is not easy to penetrate into the motives").
- brewster-0173, brewster-0174 (biography, Ch. XII): Leibnitz died 14 Nov 1716; the scholium mentioning Leibniz's calculus struck out of the 3rd edition (1725) "either by Newton or by the editor"; Brewster: "perhaps rash and ill-advised" yet justified. Biographer's view (defensive of Newton).

Unfinished work (primary)
- opticks-0000 (Advertisement I): Book III "left imperfect, not having tried all the Experiments which I intended"; crowns about the Sun and Moon left "to be farther examined"; "To communicate what I have tried, and leave the rest to others for farther Enquiry, is all my Design".
- opticks-0287 (Book III Part I): "I was then interrupted, and cannot now think of taking these things into farther Consideration"; queries "in order to a farther search to be made by others"; Query 3, rays bent "with a motion like that of an Eel".
- opticks-0345 (end of Query 31): "In this third Book I have only begun the Analysis ... leaving the Hints to be examin'd and improv'd".
- opticks-0002 (Advertisement II): the gravity Question, "not yet satisfied about it for want of Experiments".
- principia-0784 (General Scholium, last paragraph): the "most subtle Spirit" behind cohesion, electricity, light and sensation; "nor are we furnished with that sufficiency of experiments".
- principia-0002 (Author's Preface): lunar motions "being imperfect"; wishes the rest of nature could be derived the same way; "I heartily beg that what I have here done may be read with candour".
- principia-0003 (prefaces to 2nd and 3rd editions): what he kept revising: moon theory, resistance of fluids, comets (incl. comet of 1723 computed by Bradley).

Unfinished work (biography)
- brewster-0222 (Ch. XV): the Chronology of Ancient Kingdoms "nearly completed at the time of his death", published 1728, dedicated to the queen by Conduit; sixth chapter found among his papers, uncertain if meant for publication.
- brewster-0218 (Ch. XV): chronology existed "merely in separate papers ... in a state of confusion"; abstract for the Princess of Wales on condition of secrecy, then copied for Abbé Conti; Newton's caveat about errors of years (his words).
- brewster-0240 (Ch. XVI): Lexicon Propheticum "left incomplete, and has not been published".
- chittenden-0015: Method of Fluxions: intended as intro to Kinckhuysen's Algebra, abandoned; late in life he consented to publish it and "would probably have been carried into execution had not his death intervened" (Chittenden's "probably"); published 1736 by Colson.
- brewster-0270 (Ch. XVIII): even in 1725 at Kensington, explaining to Conduit how comets might replenish the sun and stars: still working on it.

Reputation / how he wanted to be seen
- chittenden-0057 and brewster-0284 (biography): the sea-shore saying, "A short time before his death". Newton's words. Both also quote Pemberton: age and fame had not made him "stiff in opinion".
- brewster-0284: Brewster: his modesty "was not founded on any indifference to the fame which they conferred"; "he knew his place as a philosopher, and was determined to assert and vindicate his rights". Useful for the host to push back ("You say pebbles, but you fought Leibniz for twenty years").
- brewster-0277 / chittenden-0041: he attributed success "not to any extraordinary sagacity, but solely to industry and patient thought" (reported speech, biographers' words).
- opticks-0000: did not want the Opticks translated "without my Consent"; published only "what I think proper to come abroad".

Last illness and death (memories up to his last days)
- brewster-0270 (Ch. XVIII): 1722, aged 80, incontinence ascribed to stone, thought incurable; gave up his carriage for a chair; declined dinners; diet of broth, vegetables, fruit; Aug 1724 passed a stone "the size of a pea"; Jan 1725 cough and lung inflammation, moved to Kensington; gout Feb 1725 improved him; 7 March conversation with Conduit on comets. Niece Catherine Barton (Mrs Conduit) ran his household for his last twenty years.
- brewster-0271 (Ch. XVIII): wanted to resign the Mint to Conduit in 1725; Conduit did the duties; went to London 28 Feb 1727 to preside at the Royal Society; boasted he had slept from eleven till eight; violent relapse; back to Kensington 4 March; Dr Mead and Dr Cheselden gave no hope; sweat ran down his face but "he never uttered a cry or a complaint"; read the newspapers on Saturday 18th; insensible that evening; died Monday 20 March 1727 between one and two in the morning, in his 85th year.
- chittenden-0050, chittenden-0058: same account; Chittenden adds the Maclaurin offer (1724, £20 a year) and his generosity: "Those, he would often remark, who gave away nothing till they died, never gave at all" (reported saying).
- chittenden-0059: Chittenden's pious eulogy (not quotable as Newton).

Burial and memorials (host only; after his death)
- brewster-0272 (Ch. XVIII): lay in state in the Jerusalem Chamber on Tuesday 28 March; buried in Westminster Abbey near the entrance to the choir, left side; pall: Lord High Chancellor, Dukes of Roxburghe and Montrose, Earls of Pembroke, Sussex, Macclesfield; chief mourner Sir Michael Newton; Bishop of Rochester read the service; relatives spent £500 on a monument (1731) in a place "often refused to the greatest of our nobility"; reliefs of youths with prism, reflecting telescope, steelyard weighing the sun and planets, a furnace, and "money newly coined"; Astronomy weeping on a globe showing the comet of 1681(sic in Brewster).
- brewster-0273: the Latin epitaph and Brewster's translation ("Let Mortals rejoice That there has existed such and so great AN ORNAMENT OF HUMAN NATURE"); 1731 medal, motto Felix cognoscere causas.
- brewster-0274: Roubiliac statue at Trinity, 1755, "Qui genus humanum ingenio superavit".
- brewster-0275: estate about £32,000 divided among eight half-nephews and nieces; Woolsthorpe sold 1732; Conduit succeeded him at the Mint.
- chittenden-0062, brewster-0289: the couplet "Nature and Nature's laws lay hid in night..." on the Woolsthorpe tablet (1798). Neither passage names Pope (see Gaps).
- brewster-0290: the fate of his papers (see 09).

## Gaps

- **Pope is not named.** The couplet appears in chittenden-0062 and brewster-0289 as part of the Woolsthorpe tablet (put up 1798), but neither passage attributes it to Alexander Pope. If the host says "Alexander Pope wrote", that is a host claim from general knowledge, not from our sources; flag it. Newton cannot know it (written for/after his death). Brewster mentions Pope only as a friend of Halifax (brewster-0208).
- **Death and funeral are after his last conscious moment.** He was insensible from the evening of 18 March (brewster-0271). Burial, monument, epitaph, statue, medal: host tells him; he reacts.
- **No last words** are recorded in the sources. Do not invent any.
- **"Shoulders of giants"** (letter to Hooke) is NOT in the indexed texts. Do not use.
- **Flamsteed:** no regret by Newton about Flamsteed is recorded in these passages; Brewster's chapter defends Newton. Do not invent remorse over Flamsteed or Hooke.
- **Regret about the Leibniz quarrel:** no passage has Newton expressing regret over it; the regret-like judgements (should have published earlier) are the biographers' (chittenden-0044, brewster-0156). Newton can say what he said (disputes cost him his quiet) but not that he regretted fighting Leibniz.
- **Pebble saying overlap:** may also be wanted for 05/06; it is listed here because it fits reputation best. Coordinate.
- **Alchemy:** only a second-hand mention (brewster-0254: Rev. Mr Law's report of furnaces and the "philosopher's tincture"; Flammel transcripts among the Portsmouth papers; Brewster says "no reason to suppose" he believed in alchymy). Huygens's journal mentions losing "by fire, his chymical laboratory" (brewster-0178, hearsay from M. Colin). Treat as thin; do not build an alchemy confession.
- **1692-93 illness:** Huygens's hearsay that he "had become insane" (brewster-0178) is disputed by Brewster (brewster-0184). If raised, Newton should not confirm madness; the sources support only that he was "very much troubled" and that he wrote the Bentley letters in that period.
- Brewster's line about "the comet of 1681" on the monument is as Brewster prints it (the Principia discusses the comet of 1680).
- Modern reputation (Einstein, being "overturned", top-scientist polls) is host material only; the guest reasons from Rule IV (07).
