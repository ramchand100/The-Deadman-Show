# The big ideas, simply

Goal: Explain the core ideas plainly, and correct what people most often get wrong about the guest.
Target: 10 min (~1500 words)

Beats:
- Host asks for the core idea as if explaining to a teenager.
- Host raises the popular misconception; guest corrects it with their own words.
- One idea the guest thinks is underrated.

## Questions

1. If you had to explain your three laws of motion to a teenager, with a stone, a horse and a rope, how would you put it? (principia-0024, -0025, -0026)
2. How did you show that the force holding the Moon in its orbit is the same thing that makes a stone fall? Walk us through the "Moon test". (principia-0578 to -0581; memory: brewster-0115 to -0118)
3. Gravity "inverse as the square of the distance": what does that mean, and how sure were you that it holds for a whole planet and not only for a point? (principia-0592, -0591, -0783)
4. A lot of people think you explained what gravity *is*. Did you? (principia-0783, principia-0565, opticks-0002, opticks-0320, opticks-0341)
5. And the apple: did it hit you on the head? (brewster-0289, chittenden-0061, brewster-0115, chittenden-0007)
6. You put a prism in a dark room and broke white light into colours. What exactly did you see, and how did the second prism prove it? (opticks-0032, -0033, -0047, -0048, -0118; brewster-0017, -0018)
7. Your "Rules of Reasoning" sound like advice for anyone. What are they, and why does Nature "affects not the pomp of superfluous causes"? (principia-0562 to -0566)
8. Underrated idea: at the end of the Opticks you left a list of open questions (the Queries) about particles, attractions and method. Why end a book with questions? (opticks-0287, -0320, -0340, -0344, -0345)

## Passages

Laws of motion
- principia-0024 (primary, Book I, Axioms, Law I): first law; projectiles, a spinning top, planets keep moving in "more free spaces".
- principia-0025 (primary, Book I, Law II): alteration of motion proportional to force; "a double force will generate double the motion".
- principia-0026 (primary, Book I, Law III): action and reaction; finger pressing a stone, horse drawing a stone on a rope. Says the law "takes place also in attractions".
- principia-0015 (primary, Book I, Scholium after Definitions): absolute vs relative time; "Absolute, true, and mathematical time ... flows equably". Background only.

Gravity, inverse square, Moon test
- principia-0578, -0579, -0580 (primary, Book III, Prop. IV): the Moon test. Moon at about 60 Earth semi-diameters; period 27d. 7h. 43'; force at Earth's surface is "60 × 60 times greater" than at the Moon; a body should fall "15 feet, 1 inch, and 1 line" (Paris measure) in one second, which matches Huygens's pendulum; therefore the Moon's force "is that very same force which we commonly call gravity" (by Rules I and II). NB: several fractional numbers are missing from the ingested text (blank gaps), so do not quote the full figures.
- principia-0581 (primary, Book III, Scholium to Prop. IV): plainer version: imagine a little moon skimming the mountain tops; it would fall like a stone, or else "fall twice as swiftly". Very good for a teenager-level explanation.
- principia-0585 (primary, Book III, Prop. VI): his own pendulum experiment: two equal round wooden boxes on 11-foot threads, one filled with wood, one with gold; also tried silver, lead, glass, sand, common salt, water, wheat; they swung together "for a long time, with equal vibrations". Weight proportional to quantity of matter. Concrete hands-on story.
- principia-0591 (primary, Book III, Prop. VII): gravity tends to all bodies, proportional to their matter; Law III means planet B is drawn to every part of A.
- principia-0592 (primary, Book III, Prop. VIII): inverse square between spheres; he admits "I was yet in doubt" whether it held accurately near a planet's surface until Props. LXXV-LXXVI satisfied him. Good honesty moment.
- principia-0000 to -0002 (primary, Author's Preface): his programme: "from the phænomena of motions to investigate the forces of nature"; suspects all phenomena may depend on attractive/repulsive forces between particles; credits Halley for pressing him to publish.
- brewster-0115, -0116 (biography, ch. XI): 1666, plague, sitting alone in the garden at Woolsthorpe; reasons gravity may reach the Moon; finds inverse square from planetary periods; first calculation fails (Earth's size taken as 60 miles per degree) and gives a result "one-sixth greater". Biographer's narration: memory, not quotes.
- brewster-0117, -0118 (biography, ch. XI): put it aside; 1679 correspondence with Hooke (falling body, ellipse); June 1682 hears of Picard's measurement of a degree at the Royal Society, redoes the 1665 calculation, becomes so agitated "he was unable to carry on the calculation" and hands it to a friend. Brewster gives no source and does not name the friend.
- chittenden-0007, -0008 (biography): same 1666 garden scene, autumn, "seated alone beneath a tree"; no apple mentioned in the narrative.

Rules of Reasoning
- principia-0562 (primary, Book III, Rule I): admit no more causes than true and sufficient; "Nature is pleased with simplicity".
- principia-0563 (primary, Rule II): same effects, same causes: breathing in man and beast, stones falling in Europe and America, kitchen fire and the sun.
- principia-0564, -0565 (primary, Rule III): universal qualities known by experiment; "dreams and vain fictions"; ends with universal gravitation, and "Not that I affirm gravity to be essential to bodies".
- principia-0566 (primary, Rule IV): induction from phenomena holds "till such time as other phænomena occur".

General Scholium
- principia-0778 (primary, General Scholium): vortices fail; comets move too freely; in Boyle's vacuum "a bit of fine down and a piece of solid gold descend with equal velocity"; gravity cannot explain the original regular positions of the orbits.
- principia-0779 (primary): "This most beautiful system of the sun, planets, and comets ..."; fixed stars placed at immense distances so they do not fall together.
- principia-0780 to -0782 (primary): God as Lord over all, dominion, "Universal Ruler"; "As a blind man has no idea of colours ...".
- principia-0783 (primary): "I frame no hypotheses"; gravity's cause not yet found; "to us it is enough that gravity does really exist".
- principia-0784 (primary): speculation about "a certain most subtle Spirit" (electric, elastic) that he declines to develop for want of experiments.

Light and colour
- opticks-0004 (primary, Book I, Part I opening): "My Design in this Book is not to explain the Properties of Light by Hypotheses, but to propose and prove them by Reason and Experiments".
- opticks-0032, -0033 (primary, Book I, Prop. II, Exper. 3): dark chamber, hole about a third of an inch in the window-shut, prism; the image on paper 18 and a half feet away was oblong, about 10 and a quarter inches long and about 2 and an eighth inches broad; prism angle 64 degrees.
- opticks-0047, -0048 (primary, Book I, Prop. II, Exper. 6): the two-boards, two-prisms experiment (what is usually called the experimentum crucis, though the Opticks does not use that name here): light most refracted by the first prism is again more refracted by the second; hence "more refrangible".
- opticks-0059 (primary, Book I, Prop. II): removing the most refrangible rays turns white to yellow, orange, red; the prism serves "only to separate" rays, not to alter colours.
- opticks-0111 (primary, Book I, Definition after Prop. II): "the Rays to speak properly are not coloured"; colour is a disposition; analogy with sound in a bell.
- opticks-0118, -0120 (primary, Book I, Prop. V): whiteness compounded of all colours; white paper held in the spectrum; intercept colours at a lens and white is lost, let them pass and it is restored.
- brewster-0017, -0018 (biography, ch. III): plain retelling of the two-board experiment; earlier he suspected rays might curve like a tennis ball struck obliquely; consequence: gave up improving refracting telescopes and turned to reflection.
- brewster-0031 (biography, ch. IV): recomposing white from painters' powders; contains a quoted line in Newton's voice about stopping a friend at the door to compare two whites (see quotes).

Queries (end of Opticks)
- opticks-0287 (primary, Book III): "I was then interrupted"; he ends with Queries "in order to a farther search to be made by others"; Qu. 1-3 (rays bent "with a motion like that of an Eel").
- opticks-0313, -0314 (primary, Book III, Qu. 28 area): rejects a dense medium filling the heavens; natural philosophy should "argue from Phænomena without feigning Hypotheses"; questions: "what hinders the fix'd Stars from falling upon one another?"
- opticks-0315 (primary, Qu. 28 end / Qu. 29): God's "Sensory"; Qu. 29 "Are not the Rays of Light very small Bodies emitted from shining Substances?"
- opticks-0320 (primary, Qu. 31 opening): particles with powers acting at a distance; "What I call Attraction may be perform'd by impulse, or by some other means unknown to me."
- opticks-0340 (primary, Qu. 31): God in the beginning formed matter in "solid, massy, hard, impenetrable, moveable Particles".
- opticks-0341 (primary, Qu. 31): active principles are not occult qualities; "their Causes only are occult".
- opticks-0344, -0345 (primary, Qu. 31 end): method of analysis before composition (synthesis); induction is "no Demonstration" but the best available; natural philosophy, perfected, would enlarge moral philosophy.
- opticks-0002 (primary, Advertisement II, 1717): he added a Query about gravity's cause "to shew that I do not take Gravity for an essential Property of Bodies".

Misconceptions
- brewster-0289 (biography, ch. XIX, Woolsthorpe memorials) and chittenden-0061: the only apple in the sources: "The celebrated apple-tree, the fall of one of the apples of which is said to have turned the attention of Newton to the subject of gravity", destroyed by wind and preserved as a chair. "is said to have" = tradition, not attested. Nothing about it hitting his head.
- brewster-0240 to -0245 (biography, ch. XVI): letters to Bentley 1692-93: innate gravity and the need for a Deity. Mostly Brewster's paraphrase; the double-quoted passages are Newton's words (see quotes). Note brewster-0241's opening quote is in the third person ("he had an eye upon ...") so it is not Newton's verbatim wording.
- brewster-0171 (biography, ch. XII): Leibnitz accused him of "reviving the occult qualities" and objected "that each particle of matter attracts every other particle". Useful as the contemporary objection the guest answers with opticks-0341.

## Exact quotes

Laws and gravity (primary)
- "Every body perseveres in its state of rest, or of uniform motion in a right line, unless it is compelled to change that state by forces impressed thereon." (principia-0024)
- "If any force generates a motion, a double force will generate double the motion, a triple force triple the motion" (principia-0025)
- "To every action there is always opposed an equal reaction" (principia-0026)
- "If you press a stone with your finger, the finger is also pressed by the stone." (principia-0026)
- "the horse (if I may so say) will be equally drawn back towards the stone" (principia-0026)
- "the force by which the moon is retained in its orbit is that very same force which we commonly call gravity" (principia-0580)
- "this little moon at the top of a mountain must either be without gravity, or fall twice as swiftly as heavy bodies are wont to do." (principia-0581)
- "I tried the thing in gold, silver, lead, glass, sand, common salt, wood, water, and wheat." (principia-0585)
- "I observed them to play together forward and backward, for a long time, with equal vibrations." (principia-0585)
- "I was yet in doubt whether that reciprocal duplicate proportion did accurately hold, or but nearly so" (principia-0592)
- "for all the difficulty of philosophy seems to consist in this—from the phænomena of motions to investigate the forces of nature, and then from these forces to demonstrate the other phænomena" (principia-0001)

Rules of Reasoning (primary)
- "We are to admit no more causes of natural things than such as are both true and sufficient to explain their appearances." (principia-0562)
- "Nature is pleased with simplicity, and affects not the pomp of superfluous causes." (principia-0562)
- "the descent of stones in Europe and in America; the light of our culinary fire and of the sun" (principia-0563)
- "We are certainly not to relinquish the evidence of experiments for the sake of dreams and vain fictions of our own devising" (principia-0564)
- "Not that I affirm gravity to be essential to bodies" (principia-0565)
- "This rule we must follow, that the argument of induction may not be evaded by hypotheses." (principia-0566)

General Scholium (primary)
- "in this void a bit of fine down and a piece of solid gold descend with equal velocity." (principia-0778)
- "This most beautiful system of the sun, planets, and comets, could only proceed from the counsel and dominion of an intelligent and powerful Being." (principia-0779)
- "This Being governs all things, not as the soul of the world, but as Lord over all" (principia-0780)
- "As a blind man has no idea of colours, so have we no idea of the manner by which the all-wise God perceives and understands all things." (principia-0782)
- "Hitherto we have explained the phænomena of the heavens and of our sea by the power of gravity, but have not yet assigned the cause of this power." (principia-0783)
- "But hitherto I have not been able to discover the cause of those properties of gravity from phænomena, and I frame no hypotheses" (principia-0783)
- "And to us it is enough that gravity does really exist, and act according to the laws which we have explained, and abundantly serves to account for all the motions of the celestial bodies, and of our sea." (principia-0783)

Light and colour (primary)
- "My Design in this Book is not to explain the Properties of Light by Hypotheses, but to propose and prove them by Reason and Experiments" (opticks-0004)
- "In a very dark Chamber, at a round Hole, about one third Part of an Inch broad, made in the Shut of a Window, I placed a Glass Prism" (opticks-0032)
- "This Image was Oblong and not Oval" (opticks-0033)
- "And those were more refracted in this Prism, which by a greater Refraction in the first Prism were more turned out of the way, and therefore for their Constancy of being more refracted are deservedly called more refrangible." (opticks-0048)
- "the whiteness of the Sun's Light is compounded of all the primary Colours mix'd in a due Proportion." (opticks-0118)
- "For the Rays to speak properly are not coloured." (opticks-0111)
- Newton's own words, quoted by Brewster (who does not name the source paper): "when I was trying this, a friend coming to visit me, I stopped him at the door, and before I told him what the colours were, or what I was doing, I asked him which of the two whites were the best, and wherein they differed!" (brewster-0031)

Queries and method (primary)
- "But I was then interrupted, and cannot now think of taking these things into farther Consideration." (opticks-0287)
- "I shall conclude with proposing only some Queries, in order to a farther search to be made by others." (opticks-0287)
- "Are not the Rays of Light in passing by the edges and sides of Bodies, bent several times backwards and forwards, with a motion like that of an Eel?" (opticks-0287)
- "Are not the Rays of Light very small Bodies emitted from shining Substances?" (opticks-0315)
- "What I call Attraction may be perform'd by impulse, or by some other means unknown to me." (opticks-0320)
- "For we must learn from the Phænomena of Nature what Bodies attract one another, and what are the Laws and Properties of the Attraction, before we enquire the Cause by which the Attraction is perform'd." (opticks-0320)
- "it seems probable to me, that God in the Beginning form'd Matter in solid, massy, hard, impenetrable, moveable Particles" (opticks-0340)
- "For these are manifest Qualities, and their Causes only are occult." (opticks-0341)
- "To tell us that every Species of Things is endow'd with an occult specifick Quality by which it acts and produces manifest Effects, is to tell us nothing" (opticks-0341)
- "As in Mathematicks, so in Natural Philosophy, the Investigation of difficult Things by the Method of Analysis, ought ever to precede the Method of Composition." (opticks-0344)
- "And although the arguing from Experiments and Observations by Induction be no Demonstration of general Conclusions; yet it is the best way of arguing which the Nature of Things admits of" (opticks-0344)
- "what hinders the fix'd Stars from falling upon one another?" (opticks-0314)
- "And to shew that I do not take Gravity for an essential Property of Bodies, I have added one Question concerning its Cause, chusing to propose it by way of a Question, because I am not yet satisfied about it for want of Experiments." (opticks-0002)

Letters to Bentley (Newton's words reproduced in a biography, Brewster)
- "To make such a system with all its motions required a cause which understood and compared together the quantities of matter in the several bodies of the sun and planets, and the gravitating powers resulting from thence" (brewster-0242)
- "argues that cause to be not blind and fortuitous, but very well skilled in mechanics and geometry." (brewster-0242)

## Gaps

- **The apple.** Neither biography tells the apple story as an event. Both mention it only once, when describing the tree at Woolsthorpe: an apple "is said to have" turned his attention to gravity (brewster-0289, chittenden-0061). Brewster's and Chittenden's own accounts of the 1666 garden scene (brewster-0115, chittenden-0007/-0008) have no apple at all. **Nothing says an apple hit his head.** Newton never mentions an apple in his own writings here. The guest can say the tradition is not his to confirm. He must not tell an apple story as a memory.
- **"Experimentum crucis".** The Latin phrase does not appear in any indexed text. The experiment itself is Opticks Book I, Prop. II, Exper. 6 (opticks-0047/-0048), and Brewster retells it (brewster-0017). If the host uses the name, the guest can accept it as the host's word, but should not quote it as his own. The original 1672 letter to the Royal Society where he used the phrase is not in our sources.
- **Moon test numbers.** In principia-0578 to -0580 many fractions (the Moon's distance, the fall in one minute in Paris feet) did not survive ingestion. They appear as blanks. Use only the complete figures: 60 semi-diameters, 27d. 7h. 43', 60 × 60, "15 feet, 1 inch, and 1 line", Huygens's seconds pendulum. Brewster's 1666 version (60 miles per degree, a result "one-sixth greater") is the biographer's account. Brewster gives no source for the 1682 Picard story or the friend who finished the calculation (brewster-0118), so treat both as biographer's narrative.
- **The 1679 Hooke letters** survive only as Brewster's summary (brewster-0117/-0118). Their text is not in our sources. Brewster says Hooke corrected Newton's spiral error. A fair guest can admit this.
- **"Hypotheses non fingo".** Only the English "I frame no hypotheses" (Motte translation) is indexed. The Latin wording is not.
- **The General Scholium is not from 1687.** Brewster says it first appeared in the second edition of 1713 (brewster-0199). The guest should not say it was in the first Principia.
- **The Bentley letters (1692-93)** survive only as Brewster's summary with a few quoted lines (brewster-0240 to -0245). The famous line calling action at a distance through a vacuum an "absurdity" is **not** in our sources. Do not use it.
- **Fluxions / calculus as an idea** is barely covered in my passages, and only from the priority-dispute side. The guest should not explain "calculus" in modern terms. Brewster ch. XII (brewster-0143 onward) covers the history if the writer needs it.
- **Modern expectations not covered:** the sources do not mention "F = ma", gravitational constant G, Newton's cradle, or the word "spectrum" used as a theory of photons. The Opticks has no wave/particle "debate" framing beyond Qu. 29 and Brewster's notes on Hooke and Huygens (brewster-0039/-0040). Note that opticks-0003 (Advertisement to the fourth edition) is the **publisher's** note, not Newton's words.
